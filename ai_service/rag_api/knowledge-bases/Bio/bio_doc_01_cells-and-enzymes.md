# Cells, membranes, and enzymes (grab-bag)

## Membrane basics
- **Phospholipid bilayer:** Amphipathic phospholipids self-assemble into bilayers in water. Hydrophobic tails face inward; hydrophilic heads face outward.
- **Fluid mosaic model:** Membranes behave like 2D fluids: lipids and many proteins can move laterally.
- **Cholesterol (in animal membranes):** Generally reduces membrane permeability and buffers fluidity (prevents too-rigid at low temp, too-fluid at high temp).

## Transport across membranes
- **Passive transport:** No direct energy input.
  - **Simple diffusion:** Down concentration gradient through membrane (e.g., small nonpolar molecules).
  - **Facilitated diffusion:** Down gradient via proteins (channels/carriers).
- **Active transport:** Requires energy (directly or indirectly).
  - **Primary active transport:** Uses ATP directly (e.g., Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase).
  - **Secondary active transport:** Uses an existing gradient (symport/antiport).

## Enzyme notes
- **Enzymes lower activation energy**; they do not change ΔG of a reaction.
- **Active site:** Binds substrates with specificity (often via shape + chemistry).
- **Competitive inhibition:** Inhibitor competes for active site; can often be overcome by adding more substrate.
- **Allosteric regulation:** Molecule binds away from active site, changing enzyme activity (often used in feedback control).

## Quick definitions
- **Denaturation:** Loss of protein structure (and usually function) due to heat, pH, or chemicals.
- **Osmosis:** Net water movement across a semipermeable membrane toward higher solute concentration.
